## The problem
Helping someone prepare for an emergency involves two people with different needs: the organiser who wants to help, and the recipient who controls their household information and progress.

## My contribution
I built and launched a responsive web application and worked across product design and implementation: planning journeys, household setup, recipient experiences, progress tracking and sharing.

## Interaction decisions visible in the product
- **Separate organiser and recipient experiences:** support coordination while keeping the recipient in control.
- **Consent-based progress sharing:** recipients choose whether to share progress and can stop.
- **Concrete actions and recovery:** checklist items explain what to prepare, support requests for help, and allow completion to be undone.
- **A printable plan:** the checklist remains useful when power or connectivity is unavailable.
- **Privacy boundaries:** private health and care information is not displayed to organisers.

## Status and scope
The public product is live at their72.com. The public website and screens were checked on 23 November 2026. These screens use a fictional household; they do not expose real household data.

THEIR72 is a preparedness planning aid, not an emergency service. This case study describes the product experience, not independently validated emergency outcomes.

The application source remains private.
